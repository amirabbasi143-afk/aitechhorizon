25 Useful ChatGPT Prompts for Students � With Examples

Updated on September 4, 2026 | Category: AI Tools | By: AI Tech Horizon Editorial Team

When used responsibly, ChatGPT is an incredible academic assistant. The key to getting good results is moving beyond basic prompts like "explain this" and giving the AI specific roles, constraints, and formats.

Here are some of the most practical prompts for students, complete with examples of when to use them.

Academic Integrity Warning ??

Never use AI to generate text that you submit as your own work. This is plagiarism, and universities use detection software that will flag it. Always check your institution's specific AI policy. Use these prompts for studying, brainstorming, and comprehension only.

1. The Feynman Technique (Simplifying Complex Topics)

When to use it: When you are reading a textbook and completely lost.

The Prompt: "Act as a friendly tutor. Explain [Complex Topic] to me using the Feynman technique. Use simple analogies related to everyday life, and ask me one question at the end to check my understanding."

Example: "Explain quantum entanglement to me using the Feynman technique..."

2. Creating a Study Schedule

When to use it: During finals week when you are overwhelmed.

The Prompt: "I have a final exam for [Subject] on [Date]. Today is [Today's Date]. I can study for 2 hours a day. Create a detailed, day-by-day study schedule breaking down these main topics: [List 4-5 topics]."

3. Socratic Questioning (Active Recall)

When to use it: When you need to test your knowledge before an exam.

The Prompt: "Act as my professor for [Subject]. Ask me one challenging, open-ended question about [Topic]. Wait for my response. Do not provide the answer. After I respond, grade my answer out of 10, correct my mistakes, and then ask the next question."

4. Summarizing Long Readings

When to use it: When you have a 30-page PDF to read before a seminar.

The Prompt: "Below is a text from my required reading. Provide a 500-word summary. Then, extract the 3 most important arguments the author is making, and suggest 2 critical questions I could ask about this text in class. [Paste Text]"

5. Thesis Brainstorming

When to use it: When you have to write an essay but don't know what to argue.

The Prompt: "I am writing a 5-page paper on [Book/Historical Event]. Suggest 3 highly debatable, specific thesis statements I could explore. For each statement, list two pieces of evidence I would need to find to support it."

Conclusion

By giving ChatGPT specific roles and asking for interactive feedback (rather than just asking it to do the work for you), you can drastically improve your comprehension and study efficiency.
